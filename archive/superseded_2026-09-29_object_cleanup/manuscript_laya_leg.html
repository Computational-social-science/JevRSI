2. The instrument

2.1 Two floors, kept separate

A loop that compares two runs needs the noise of the difference between two runs, not the noise of one run. We measure the two sources separately so that neither can hide inside the other.

2.2 The threshold, and the two mistakes that were made building it

The accept threshold composes both floors and allocates the family-wise level over the loop's declared horizon. Two defects were found and fixed while calibrating it, and both are reported because both changed the number:

  1. Wrong variance. The first threshold used the spread of a single arm where the rule compares two noisy runs. That inflated the threshold: on the development split it was 6.05 pp against a defensible 2.39 pp — a factor of 2.5.
  2. No horizon. The driver then hard-coded the single-decision factor, so the rule behaved as if the loop would make exactly one decision whatever its horizon. For a long-running loop this is the difference between a valid rule and a decorative one.

The horizon is not decoration either. Because the allocated per-decision level is antitone in the horizon, understating it is a validity failure while overstating it is merely conservative — an asymmetry that is proved, not asserted (Section 6).

2.3 Does the rule work? An empirical audit under a true null

The five seed replicates are, by construction, pure noise relative to one another: they differ only by seed. Their ten pairwise differences are therefore ten true-null trials, and the accept rule can be run on them.

This is the first substantive result: the obvious rule accepts noise more often than it accepts signal when the effect scale is small, and the measured floors are what make that visible.

2.4 A non-determinism result that limits the ledger's meaning

Five re-runs of one seed, identical configuration, produced five different scores, spread 0.70 pp. Against five distinct seeds, the within-seed standard deviation was 0.278 pp (df = 4) and the between-seed 0.400 pp (df = 4), a ratio of 0.696 with a 95% interval of [0.225, 2.157]. The interval straddles the pre-registered boundary, so the reading is INDETERMINATE: the point estimate suggests a large share of the run-to-run spread is non-determinism rather than seed choice, but the data cannot separate them. The honest consequence is that a ledger row is not a reproducible datum unless the seed is stated and the run is repeatable.

5. Negative results and retractions

Recorded so that the same ground is not covered twice.

  1. The step axis is dead. 5.6× compute → +2.17 pp (Section 3.2).
  2. The instrument cannot see the search space. Threshold ≥ entire observable range (Section 3.3).
  3. Vocabulary was not the handicap. Hypothesised, measured, refuted: 1.04–1.07× token cost.
  4. A retracted plateau claim. A +0.17 pp step was once narrated as a plateau and then retracted when it proved to be below the project's own measured noise. The plateau was later re-established on two independent steps and 5.6× compute — but the retraction stands as the correct response to the earlier, weaker evidence.
  5. A retracted arithmetic error. An extrapolation quoted 4,369 hours per generation; the correct figure is 3.4–4.4 hours. The error was a unit mislabel, and it was found by re-deriving rather than by review.
  6. Same-seed runs are not identical. Spread 0.70 pp, ratio indeterminate (Section 2.4).
  7. A degenerate threshold. The first mechanism test used an absolute cut that 2 of 2,000 items satisfied (Section 3.5).
  8. A falsified proof. An early Lean scaffold contained a placeholder dressed as a proof. It was deleted; no unproved statement is permitted to appear in the formalization.

6. Formalization

The parts of the framework that are mathematical objects are stated in Lean 4 and checked against the kernel rather than asserted. The statistical core, in lean/JevRSI/AcceptRule.lean, contains four closed theorems with no sorry:

  • the per-decision level allocated over a horizon is antitone in that horizon, so a longer planned run can only lower the per-decision level — the formal content of the defect in Section 2.2;
  • the same ordering is strict for a genuinely longer horizon, which is why the horizon must be declared rather than assumed away;
  • the combined variance is monotone in each floor, so an underestimated floor yields an anti-conservative rule;
  • the threshold, as a non-negative multiple of that variance, is monotone in the floors as well.

Writing these found a real constraint rather than confirming a guess: the antitone statement is false without a positivity hypothesis, because field division sends α / 0 to zero rather than to infinity. The hypothesis is load-bearing.

Scope of the formalization — stated plainly A formal proof can only cover a mathematical object. The measured floors are measurements, not theorems; the benchmark's published boundary is a documentary claim, not a proposition. The kernel can therefore certify "given these floors and this horizon, the rule's level is what we say it is" — it cannot certify the floors or the boundary. Conflating the two would dress an empirical premise as a proved one.
