A self-evolving decision model on one consumer GPU:
measuring the instrument before trusting the loop

JevRSI · Day-1 measurement report · 2026-09-29
Object. A 24/7 self-evolving optimisation loop for a Jev-style decision model under two hard constraints: one RTX 4070 (12 GB, 150 W) and zero external API. Seed: AgentJev-0.6B (Qwen3-0.6B backbone, permutation-equivariant scoring head) with per-primitive L1 temperature calibration.
Status. Baseline and instrument measured. The loop has not run. Every number below is traced to a file under measurement/ or loop/.
This is not a success claim. No accepted improvement exists yet. What is offered is the instrument, three measured findings that constrain the design, and one environmental defect found and fixed.

Abstract

An autonomous optimisation loop is only as trustworthy as the instrument that decides what to keep. This report establishes that instrument for a Jev-style decision model on a single consumer GPU, and records three measurements that change how the loop must be built. First, the seed's calibration and its proper scoring rules move in opposite directions: the shipped L1 temperatures improve soft cross-entropy and Brier while making expected calibration error worse, on all three question types. Second, expected calibration error is gameable on this checkpoint — a temperature of 0.5 lowers it twelve-fold while both proper scoring rules degrade substantially, so a loop optimising ECE would have kept that configuration and reported a large calibration win. Third, the proposal's premise that 4-bit quantisation is required on a 12 GB card does not hold for a 0.6B seed: full-parameter training genuinely does not fit (13.26 GiB against 11.99 GiB), but BF16 plus a rank-16 adapter peaks at 7.80 GiB with 4.20 GiB to spare. Quantisation is therefore a numerical decision rather than a memory one, because quantisation error enters the run-to-run noise floor that sets the accept threshold. We also report a defect in which an agent runtime's PYTHONPATH silently shadowed a pinned dependency and made the environment unrunnable, with a one-variable fix and a regression test.

1. The object, and the one decision that was forced

The target is a fully local, multi-fidelity controller (Select → Mutate → Train → Evaluate → Archive → Persist) that searches a configuration space for a model beating the L1-calibrated seed on a frozen held-out set, under a six-week wall-clock budget at a 150 W cap.

One design decision was settled by measurement rather than preference, and it is recorded here because it changes the shape of every later experiment. The proposal specifies a three-way split — 30 proxy, 80 medium, 60 frozen tasks. On this benchmark the independent unit of analysis is the case, not the question: the five questions of a case are not independent. The measured scaling law confirms it, since 400 cases give a standard error of 1.067 pp and 120 cases give 2.140 pp, against 1.948 pp predicted by 1/√ncases.

Under that law, the proposal's medium set of 80 tasks is roughly 16 cases, giving a standard error near 5.33 pp and, with a Bonferroni correction over a thousand decisions, a decision threshold of 21.63 pp. The usable headroom from a 0.8150 starting point is about 20 pp. A threshold larger than the entire available range cannot distinguish a real improvement from noise, at any number of cycles: more cycles make the correction larger, not smaller.

Decision (D1, project owner): use the official split — 960 train, 120 dev, 120 calibration, 400 test — verified case-by-case against the protocol manifest. Its dev threshold is 12.38 pp, which sits below the usable headroom, so a decision is possible. What is given up is the three-way partition itself; the intent of the proposal's Rule 1, that the frozen set is touched only at pre-registered checkpoints, is preserved.

2. The instrument

A loop needs a threshold, and a threshold needs a noise model. Two floors are involved, and they behave differently under transfer: one belongs to the benchmark, the other to the optimiser. Keeping them apart is not pedantry — it is the difference between a threshold that is valid and one that was quietly borrowed.

QuantityMeasured onValueDoes it transfer to this seed?
Floor A — measurement sampling: case-level cluster bootstrap over the 400-case frozen split this split, any modelSE 1.067 pp, design effect 1.384 yes it is a property of the split
Floor B — run-to-run: SD of the final score across seeds differing only by seed a different training path: laya-multilingual 322M, full-parameter, 5 seeds SD 0.40 pp (df = 4) no it is a property of the optimiser

Floor A travels with the benchmark: it is the variability of the 400-case split itself, so any model scored on that split inherits it. Floor B travels with the training path, the quantisation scheme and the seed. The calibration file says so in its own words — "Re-derive Floor B on its own seed, because 0.40 pp is a property of this pipeline's training path. Do not inherit it silently."

The 12.38 pp figure is therefore provisional, and it errs in the optimistic direction. It was derived by placing the borrowed 0.40 pp in a variance term added in quadrature, so the true threshold can only be larger once this seed's own Floor B is measured. A threshold that is a lower bound is the dangerous kind: it admits a threshold, arms the accept rule, and looks rigorous. The code refuses to let that happen by accident — loop/epsilon.py exits non-zero rather than deriving a threshold from a Floor B it was not given, and the 12.38 pp value exists only because it was passed explicitly.

The same applies to the 3.11 pp test threshold and to the accept-rule audit in the next subsection: both were measured on the other training path. The rule they test is general — a criterion is a property of the rule, not of the model — but their numeric values are not this seed's, and a threshold on the frozen split cannot be quoted at all until a paired comparison and a Floor B exist for it.

2.1 The threshold rule, and the audit that justifies it

The threshold follows from the two floors, a replica term for the parent's own variance, and a Bonferroni correction over the loop's whole horizon — not just the current decision: 

ε = zbonferroni · sqrt( SEpaired² · (1 + replicas) + floorB² )

The correction is not optional. A loop that runs a thousand decisions at a nominal 5% spends a family-wise error rate of 1 − 0.951000, which is 1.0 to three decimals.

That claim is auditable rather than asserted. Five replicates differing only by seed yield ten unordered pairs, and every one is a trial under a true null — any difference is noise by construction. Against those ten trials the naive rule, accept if better, fires on 5; the threshold rule fires on 0. Measured on the other training path, so the counts are that arm's rather than this seed's, but the comparison is about the rule and the rule is the same.

A criterion that accepts half of pure noise cannot support a success claim. This is the single measurement that most constrains the loop's design, and it is why the proposal's own success criterion — "the lower bound of a bootstrap 95% CI is strictly positive" — is replaced before any cycle runs rather than after.

3. Three findings that change the design

3.1 Temperature cannot move Top-1, so an accuracy-based fitness is blind to the top-priority family

Dividing logits by a positive temperature is a monotone transform, so softmax(z/T) and softmax(z) have the same argmax on every item. Measured: raw and the shipped L1 temperatures both give accuracy 0.8150 and chance-corrected skill 0.7326, to the digit.

The proposal ranks calibration strategies as priority 0. A selection signal built on accuracy is therefore structurally incapable of seeing them, and would report a flat curve for a family it is supposed to be optimising.

The per-type breakdown agrees: ECE worsens on boolean (+0.0087), choice (+0.0073) and score (+0.0156). The shipped temperatures were fitted to minimise soft cross-entropy — the file records that objective and its achieved value per type — and they succeed at it. They were never fitted to ECE.

The proposal's Rule 1 states that success is "the final elite beats the L1-calibrated seed", without naming the metric. Given the above, "beats" is achievable on soft cross-entropy and unachievable on ECE, because the L1 seed is itself the worse configuration under ECE. A fitness that averages the two receives signals of opposite sign, and the loop's decisions then depend on a weighting nobody wrote down.

3.3 A fitness contract, and the trap it closes

loop/fitness.py makes the choice explicit and machine-checked. The decision metric is soft cross-entropy, a strictly proper scoring rule, which cannot be improved by collapsing toward the marginal or by sharpening: both are penalised. Accuracy and Brier act as a veto — a candidate may not buy the primary by degrading either. ECE and score MAE are reported and never decisive.

The veto is deliberately not a weighted term. A weight is an unwritten hyperparameter, and this project has already been bitten by one: the proposal's undefined fitness.

Writing the contract surfaced a bug worth recording. The guard metrics initially had no declared direction, so the code defaulted them to "higher is better" and the Brier veto rejected the shipped L1 for improving Brier from 0.0491 to 0.0476. Every metric now declares its direction explicitly and an undeclared metric raises rather than defaulting. A guard with an implicit direction is worse than no guard: it blocks correct work while appearing to protect against something.

4. Hardware: the quantisation premise does not hold

The proposal argues that 4-bit quantisation is "the only parameter-efficient path compatible with 12 GB". That conflates two claims, and measurement separates them.

ConfigurationFootprintVersus 11.99 GiB
Full-parameter AdamW (fp32 weights + grads + m,v, plus activations) 13.26 GiBdoes not fit by 1.27 GiB
BF16 base + LoRA r16 all-linear, measured over three real steps 7.80 GiB peakfits with 4.20 GiB spare

The seed's true parameter count is 751.6M, not 0.6B: the vocabulary contributes 151936 × 1024 = 155.6M embeddings. A rank-16 adapter across all seven projections of all 28 layers (196 adapters, 10.09M trainable, 1.3% of the model) trains at about 1.3 s per step at sequence length 2048, with loss falling from 13.392 to 12.87 over three steps. The step time is quoted to one decimal because it is not reproducible more finely than that: three runs of this probe gave 1.28, 1.31 and 1.34 s, a spread of 4.7% on a quantity that no reader should treat as a two-digit fact.

So the first claim holds and the second does not follow. Four-bit would save under one gibibyte on a card with four to spare. The real cost of quantisation here is numerical, not spatial: 4-bit rounding of the frozen base injects error into every forward pass, that error becomes part of Floor B, Floor B sets ε, and a larger ε means fewer detectable improvements. The proposal lists quantisation among "settings treated as a fixed baseline", which treats as free a choice that in fact sets how much noise the accept rule must tolerate. Whether to use it is therefore an empirical question with a pre-registered criterion, to be settled by measuring Floor B on both arms — and until then, no threshold may cite a Floor B from the quantised path.

5. An environmental defect, and why it is reported

Partway through the baseline the evaluator began failing on every invocation: 

ImportError: tokenizers>=0.22.0,<=0.23.0 is required for a normal functioning of this module,
but found tokenizers==0.23.1.

Nothing had been reinstalled, and the package directories carried timestamps months old. The cause is that the agent runtime exports a PYTHONPATH containing its own tools environment, whose tokenizers 0.23.1 precedes the project's correct 0.22.2 on sys.path. The installed versions satisfy the requirement; the wrong one simply arrives first.

This is reported because the failure mode invites the wrong response. Reinstalling transformers or pinning tokenizers would trade a path collision for a version conflict, in an environment whose pinned versions are exactly what makes the measurements reproducible. The fix is one variable — unset PYTHONPATH — wrapped in loop/run_clean.sh, after which the baseline reproduces digit for digit and runs faster than before.

7. Claim boundary

What is established: the instrument, its calibration on this benchmark, an empirical audit of the accept rule under a true null, three measurements that constrain the design, one rejected design decision with its arithmetic, and one environmental defect with a verified fix.

What is not established: that the loop improves anything. It has not run. No success criterion has been evaluated, because the threshold that would evaluate it requires a Floor B that has not yet been measured on this seed.

A plateau, if that is the outcome, will be released under the same standard as a win: the full failure archive, the accept-rule audit, and the instrument that produced the verdict. The instrument is the contribution that survives either outcome, and it is the part that can be checked by someone else.
